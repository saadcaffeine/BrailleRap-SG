Braille embossers, built and supported in Singapore.

BrailleRAP embosses Braille, maps and drawings onto ordinary paper. Send a page from your phone over WiFi, or from a desktop app on your computer. It's fully open source, so it's made here, fixed here and costs far less.

Print from the phone in your pocket.

The embosser runs its own WiFi network with a print page built in. Any phone, tablet or laptop with a browser can use it, with no app to install and no account to create.

  1. Join the embosser's WiFiIt appears like any other network on your phone.
  2. Open the print pageType 192.168.0.1/braille.html into the browser.
  3. Type your textIt's translated to English Braille (UEB), with a live preview of every dot.
  4. Press PrintThe page goes to the embosser's SD card, so printing keeps going even if your phone sleeps. Status lights and a progress bar show homing, paper loading and embossing.

Desktop software for documents and drawings.

Two free apps for Windows and Linux go further than the phone page. Both translate with LibLouis, the open Braille library used by screen readers worldwide.

AccessBrailleRAP: text and documents

Type text or open a document, pick a Braille standard and emboss. It's designed for accessibility first, and tested with the NVDA screen reader.

  • 200 Braille standards, including tables for English, Chinese, Malay and Tamil
  • Imports text from office documents
  • Interface available in 9 languages

AccessBrailleRAP on GitHub

DesktopBrailleRAP: images and illustrations

Import any SVG drawing and turn it into something you can feel. Lines become rows of raised dots, and colours become tactile textures, with Braille labels placed anywhere on the page.

  • Dots follow vector paths and outlines
  • Fill and stroke colours map to dot patterns
  • Scale, rotate, align and preview before embossing

DesktopBrailleRAP on GitHub

More than Braille.

A dot is a mark you can see and touch. Artists, designers and students use BrailleRAP to make tactile prints, patterns and illustrations: work meant to be read by hand as much as by eye.

Every dot position is plain G-code, so you can also generate artwork with your own code and emboss it directly.

Open source, from frame to firmware.

Every drawing, part file and line of code is published. That's what makes it practical to build and support here, rather than import.

Made locally
Laser-cut and 3D-printed parts with standard motors and electronics, fabricated and assembled in Singapore.
Supported locally
Repairs, spare parts, setup and training from people nearby, not from an overseas service centre. Nothing is locked or proprietary.
A fraction of the cost
An order of magnitude less than commercial Braille embossers, so a school or community group can afford more than one.

BrailleRAP is an open-source Braille embosser created by the BrailleRAP project. This Singapore build uses MarlinBraille2 firmware on an MKS TinyBee board with ESP3D WiFi, AccessBrailleRAP, DesktopBrailleRAP and the BrailleRAP SG web interface, which is based on BrailleRapUI by Ondrej Kyjanek.
